# Layout engines - where cards go

`Layout::Engine` is the interface (D-10); `FlowEngine` is the engine the dashboard has always had,
and **the arranger**, `Layout::arrange()` (K105, K109, K120, K121), places a page's solo cards and
groups. **The other engines below - packing, domain weight, hero region, orbital - are proposals,
not built.**

## The interface

`include/Layout/Layout.h`, `src/Layout/Layout.cpp` - pure C++, no LVGL, Arduino or ESP-IDF, tested on
the PC in `host-tests/test/test_layout`.

- A **`Container`** is a grid of units (columns x rows; a dashboard cell is 2 x 2 units at the
  default subdivision).
- An **`Item`** has the span it would like (`prefSpanX/Y`) and the least it will accept
  (`minSpanX/Y`), an optional pinned spot (`col`, `row`) and a `priority`; `present = false` is an
  empty slot.
- An **`Engine`** takes the container and the items and writes a **`Place`** for each - where it
  went and at what span, or that it did not fit; `pinFail` says a pin was refused, and stays set
  across calls so it can be reported.
- **`Events`** tells what the engine decided as it decides (a pin refused, an item dropped), for a
  log.
- **`FlowEngine`**: pins first, then reading order, the widest span that fits at the first free
  spot, and while anything does not fit, the lowest priority is dropped (ties: the later one) and
  the whole layout tried again. The page flows with it whenever the arranger has no answer yet.

`CardPage` turns its cards into items, asks its engine, and lays out the places that come back
(`src/Cards/CardPage.cpp`). **A new arrangement is a new class implementing `Layout::Engine`**, with
host tests for its properties before it draws anything.

## A cluster is a container inside an item

On the page, a cluster (a group shown as a block, `group-cards.md`) is one block. Inside it, its
tiles are laid out by `Layout::tileGrid()` (`include/Layout/Cluster.h`): the biggest touch target
first (a tile's shorter side), then the squarest tile, then the fewest empty places; a short last
row's tiles share the row's whole width. The arranger may choose the tiles across (`tc`).
`Layout::clusterSpan()` is how many cells a cluster asks for when the flow engine places it: the
fewest cells in which every tile is at least `minTile` each way, the wider block among equals;
when no block holds the tiles, the group shows as its one-cell solo card (K92).

## The group view's tiles: one capped size (K159, K162)

The group view (`ha-cards.md` §3.2) does not stretch tiles to a page's rows. **`Layout::cappedGrid(n,
w, h, gap, cap)`** (`TileCap { maxW, maxH, tall, wide }`) chooses the columns whose tile - capped at
`C_TILE_MAX_W` x `C_TILE_MAX_H`, never wider than `wide` times its height - is largest, then fewest
empty places, then the shape closest to the box; every tile is one size. **`cappedTileAt()`**
places tile `i` with the spare room spread evenly between and around the tiles (`evenGap`:
`max((room - count*size) / (count+1), gap)`), a short last row centred on the same spacing.
Host-tested in `host-tests/test/test_cluster`.

## The arranger

`include/Layout/Arranger.h`, `src/Layout/Arranger.cpp`: a page's blocks - its solo cards and its
groups - each in any shape its limits allow, on a grid of **units** (the device's own, a cell's
halves), so a group can be 2.5 cells wide and a row 1.5 cells tall. Deterministic: the same blocks
on the same screen give the same page. The code is a line-for-line port of the Layout Playground
(`host-tests/tools/playground.html`), and the host tests' expected values are the playground's own
answers (`host-tests/test/test_arranger`, generated by `host-tests/tools/gen_arranger_tests.py`):
the device's House blocks, the playground's, the 1060's and the Fleet page on every board, with
pinned grids too.

### The rules it follows (K109, K120, K121)

Every entity the same size - each solo card and each group's tiles, the size being the side of the
square with its area, a card's label or a group's header and padding paid. Solo cards square; tiles
not too far from square, more lightly; groups wider than tall on a landscape page; groups with as
many tiles the same size; a little empty space rather than unequal sizes; blocks kept near their
list order. Limits that are never crossed: a solo card no smaller than one card of the P4_5 at 7 x 3
and no taller for its width; a tile no smaller than one of a 3-wide, one-cell group on the 4B at
4 x 4 (`arrRef()`). Solo cards are no bigger than the page's entities (`big`) and groups grow before
solo cards do.

### Shapes, grids and search

- **A solo card's shapes**: any w x h units within the limits, its preferred span (`ArrBlock::aspect`,
  1.5 for the Fleet page's 3 x 2-unit Free Heap and Uptime) being what square means. Its box may be
  up to 1.5 times taller than the card may be; the card is drawn at its tallest allowed shape,
  centred, and the height it leaves costs as empty space.
- **A group's shapes**: any w x h units with its tiles in the arrangement (`tc` across x `tr` down,
  no empty row) that costs least; each tile at least `arrRef()`'s tile, wearing the device's header
  and padding (`C_HDR_H`, `C_PAD`, `C_TILE_GAP`). Tiles stacked more rows than columns pay the
  wide cost again (a horizontal group over a vertical one).
- **Kept per block and grid**: a shape that costs more than a smaller one inside it plus the empty
  units between is dropped; the ten cheapest stay and the tightest after them (`ARR_SHAPES_CHEAP` + 1;
  without the tightest, a narrow pinned page can have no answer).
- **Grids**: units across from 2 x (the board's columns - 1) to 2 x (its columns + 2), down 2 x (its
  rows -/+ 1). A count the page pins (the system panel's Col / Row) is the only one tried that way.
- **Search**: fill the first empty unit, in reading order, with one of the next three unplaced
  blocks in each of its shapes, or leave it empty; branch and bound, rows as bit masks, two passes
  per grid (against the page's equal share, then against the average the first pass found). A grid
  of more than 192 units is skipped (the search recurses a level a unit). At most 24 blocks a page;
  more flow.
- **Steps per pass per grid**: `ARR_BUDGET` 20,000 in the host tests (the playground's);
  **`ARR_BUDGET_BOARD` 7,000 on the boards** (`?arranger=budget`), which found the 4B's House page in
  half the time with the same page and score (K126).

### The cost (the owner's weights, tuned in the playground; `ArrWeights`)

| Weight | Default | Charges |
|---|---|---|
| `size` | 75 | each entity of a different size: `size` x log(size / target)^2 |
| `square` | 30 | a solo card far from square: 4 x `square` x log(h/w)^2; a tile 0.4 of that |
| `wide` | 20 | a group taller than wide: 2 x `wide` x log(h/w) |
| `twin` | 70 | groups with as many tiles but a different size: `twin` x abs(log area ratio) |
| `gap8` | 11 | an empty unit, per entity-sized area it wastes (deliberately cheap: "I'd prefer small amounts of dead space over grossly disproportionate units per tile") |
| `order` | 4 | each block placed ahead of an earlier one |
| `big` | 60 | a solo card bigger than the page's entities: `big` x log(size / target)^2 on top of `size` |

An empty tile slot in a group costs 8.

### The finish (`finishPage()`)

1. **Trim** (units): a solo card taller than near square (1.12 of its own shape) gives up units at
   its top while that brings it nearer square, never flatter than 0.85; far too flat, it gives up
   units in pairs across.
2. **Groups grow into the spare height first** (K121): each band holding a group takes an equal
   share of the page's spare height, each group in it growing to its band's height plus that share,
   never past square tiles. Solo cards keep their size.
3. **The rest is spread, in pixels**: blocks whose rows overlap make a band and the bands spread
   down the page; in each band, blocks whose columns overlap make a column, the columns spread
   across the band, each column centred in the band's height. "Spread around" is the spare in n
   parts, half of one at each edge, a whole one between. The device draws each block at its
   rectangle (`CardPage::placeArranged()`), off the unit grid.

### On the page

- **Run when a page's content changes, and kept** (`Cards/PageArranger`, K105, K167): a page is
  built often (on arrival, D-9), so the arranger never runs in a build. Its answer is kept in the
  settings file as `pages.<slug>.layout`: `v5 <fingerprint> <C>x<R> <height>` and each block's
  `c,r,w,h,tc` in units. The fingerprint (`PageArranger.cpp`) covers what is on the page: the blocks,
  their ids, tiles and aspect, whether a group has a header, the page's width, the gap, the label
  type, the board's grid and the pins, and the weights - not the page's height, a group's unpinned
  members or how many sensors a header holds. A build whose fingerprint matches places every block
  from the answer and runs the finish again. One that does not flows with `FlowEngine` and starts
  the arranger **on a task of its own** (idle priority, core 0, 24 KB stack), then builds the page again once the answer is
  kept, with "Optimizing page view..." on screen meanwhile (K183). The screen never waits. A page
  the arranger finds no layout for flows and is not tried again until its blocks or the weights
  change.
- **Height** (K167): a page built in a taller room (the system header hidden) keeps its cards'
  size and gives each unit row an even share of the extra height, half above and half below; in a
  shorter one the cards are fitted to it. Card margins (`system.ui.margins`, 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 % of
  `UIGrid::GAP`) are the gap the search plans with, so changing them arranges once.
- **Pins**: Col / Row pin a count for the page (saved as `cols` / `rows`); the arranger searches
  the other. **Auto** takes both pins away. The board's own grid (`UI::grid()`: 5 x 3 on the P4_5,
  4 x 3 on the 4B) is where an unpinned search starts.
- **Tile height** (K122, K124, K126): drawn at the page, not searched. Tiles are drawn no taller
  than 1.1 times their width (`C_TILE_TALL`) with 1.5 mm kept off top and foot (`C_TILE_FOOT`, the
  whole of it for a tile 18 mm or taller, `C_TILE_FOOT_AT`). The page's tile height is the shortest
  near-square height any group on it draws; a group whose own is taller draws halfway down to it. A
  solo card on a page with groups has a body no taller than that height, centred where the
  arranger put it, its value in the tiles' face.
- **Tuning from the PC**: `/settings?arranger=<size|square|wide|twin|gap8|order|big|budget>&v=<number>`
  (empty: the default) - the next build arranges again. What the last run did: the System Doctor's
  page report and `/popup`'s first lines.
- **Cost on the boards**: the search is bounded by its budget and runs in the background. A
  page that hits the budget (a big page, the P4_5's Fleet page of 16 cards) takes seconds; a pinned
  grid takes milliseconds. `WS_S3_4B` compiles and is untimed (its display is locked up, #69).

### Not built

Locking a layout by hand (Auto, or Col / Row until it looks right, is all there is: K120); a
priority order by domain (`FUTURE_IMPROVEMENTS.md`); a one-cell group of sensors (K103:
`ClusterGeom::oneCell` exists and nothing sets it, and the arranger's shapes do not offer one); a
stability term (each page is arranged afresh).

## The packing ("bento") engine - a proposal

What it must be: **dense** (no holes a smaller card could fill), **deterministic** and **stable**
(one card more or less moves as few others as possible - a layout that reshuffles itself is worse
than one with a hole).

How: items sorted by area, largest first, ties by declaration order; each placed where it leaves
the least unusable space (a maximal-rectangles packer on the unit grid), trying spans from preferred
down to least; and stability from the last layout - each item first tries its previous place
(kept per card id, K33, in the settings file), and only those that no longer fit are packed afresh.
Priority still decides who leaves when nothing fits. Testable off the board: the same input twice
gives the same output; one item added moves at most the items after it.

## The domain weight map - a proposal

Which cards earn space: a weight per card - its domain's (climate and media heavier than a lone
sensor), times how much it is used (taps counted in the settings file) - turned into a target area,
spans chosen within each card's least and preferred. The weights are data (a table beside the
tokens, a page able to override them), never code.

## The hero region ("primary-region page") - a proposal

One hero region takes a fixed share of the page - a 2 x 2 at the top left, or a column - and the
rest of the page is flowed or packed around it: the container split into regions, each region its
own engine run. A card is put in the region by a `primary` flag in its spec.

The first heroes the owner wants are a music player and **NINA's multi-face clock**
(`ESP32-P4-NINA-Display`, `REFERENCE_PROJECTS.md`; K81, K82). The clock is designed for a square or
round screen, so a hero region needs an **aspect** as well as a share. Two consequences:

- the region's size comes from the page - the largest square the page can give without starving the
  rest - and the hero card draws to it, the way the card window draws to its window (`pm()`);
- NINA's code comes with its author's permission for non-commercial use, and that condition travels
  with anything taken from it (`REFERENCE_PROJECTS.md`).

## Orbital - a proposal

A page arranged around a centre - the room as one large card, its devices around it, heaviest
nearest: positions by distance from the centre cell, ring by ring, clockwise from the top. For the
large panels; on a grid too small for a ring it falls back to flow.
