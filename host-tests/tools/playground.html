Layout Playground

Pick a board, group entities into clusters, and watch the arranger lay out the page with no dead space where it can manage that. Drag a card or cluster onto another to reorder them. Drag a member tile within its cluster to reorder members; the others shift along and wrap to the next row. Everything here is a throwaway browser sketch: the sizes are millimetre estimates and nothing has been measured on glass.

Version 8 (2026-10-20, round six): your rules, in units. Every block is placed on a grid of units - the device's own, a cell's halves - in any shape its limits allow, so a cluster can be 2.5 cells wide and a row 1.5 cells tall. The search is paid to make every entity the same size (each solo card and each tile: the side of the square with its area, label and header paid), solo cards square, clusters wider than tall, clusters with as many tiles the same size, and to waste little - but an empty unit is cheap: a little margin beats unequal sizes. Your limits are never crossed: a solo card no smaller than one of the P4_5 at 7 x 3, and no taller for its width; a tile no smaller than one of a 3 x 1 cluster on the 4B at 4 x 4. Clusters wear the device's header. Spare unit rows by a solo card become even margins.

Your House (round 5) is the page from your screenshots. On the P4_5 v8 lands on what you said should happen at 5 x 2: Kitchen across the top, Outside and Office sharing the bottom row 2.5 cells each, and Doors a near-square card with a margin - every entity 17 to 19 mm. The Clusters box gives each block's units per entity, your measure. v7, v5 and v4 are still there to compare.

Version 7 (2026-10-19, round five): units. The page's cells divide into units, as the device's already do (dashboard.md section 2: two across and two down a cell, the Fleet page's 1.5-cell cards). The arranger still places blocks on cells; then a finish moves each solo card within the room around it, in units, and never touches a cluster. A card taller than it is wide gives up units at its top, so it is nearer square and its hero sits lower, nearer the tiles beside it; when one unit would leave it too flat, a cell is cut into more units down (Auto picks the fewest that gets every card near square). Spare rows above or below a card, such as an empty row under it, become even margins: the card is centred in them, the odd unit going below (up and down only; centred across an empty row, a card wandered away from its neighbours). Empty units are hatched after the finish. Untick Finish to see the page as the arranger left it.

Version 6 (2026-10-19, round four): finer cells. The page is a finer grid of cells. A solo card covers a square of cells and a cluster's tile covers another, set separately, so a cluster can be given more of the page than the cards beside it (your "1x against 0.75x"). Clusters wear no label and no header, so a card pays for its label inside its own square and a cluster keeps all of its box. Tiles are drawn as cards are, and the arranger is paid to keep every tile's icon the size of a card's icon and every tile the shape of a card. A cluster may grow by one cell to close a gap; a card never changes size.

What it shows on this House page (16 entities): with tiles a third bigger than cards (4:3) nothing fits except on the 7-inch board, because every tile then needs more page than a card does. Equal squares fit five of the seven boards with no gaps, and tile icons land within 7% of card icons: a card's label costs about what a cluster's padding does. (The 4.3-inch landscape and 3.5-inch boards need a smaller smallest card, under Knobs, or a second page.) Bare tile icons (no ring) let a tile two-thirds of a card's size carry the same size icon, which fits all seven boards and frees enough room for every icon on the page to grow, by about 15% on the P4_5. Try the three buttons under Arranger.

Version 5 (2026-10-19): a new arranger. Version 4 placed blocks first-come-first-served and then let each cluster in turn grab the empty cells beside it, so it missed layouts that needed another order and the first cluster grabbed most of the spare room. Version 5 tries every way of tiling the page: it fills the first empty cell with one of the next few blocks, in every shape that holds its tiles, or leaves it empty, and keeps the cheapest page.

Spare room is shared out instead of grabbed. A cluster pays for room beyond what its tiles comfortably need, and the charge grows faster than the room, so two clusters one cell bigger each cost less than one cluster two cells bigger. Long thin clusters pay too. A one-cell cluster is allowed when all its members fit as tiles; it has no header and wears its card label (K97). Switch Arranger to v4 to compare. The weights are starting guesses for you to tune.
